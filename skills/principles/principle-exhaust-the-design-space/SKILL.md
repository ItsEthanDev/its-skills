# Exhaust the Design Space

When a decision has no established precedent, confidence in the first idea is not evidence that it is the best. Compare alternatives that differ in a consequential way, not cosmetic variations of one design.

Use evidence proportionate to the uncertainty: sketches, analysis, examples, or experiments. A prototype is useful when experience or runtime behavior determines the answer; it is not required when the constraints already distinguish the options.

Judge alternatives against the actual question, constraints, and user experience. Favor the one whose tradeoffs are supported by evidence. A fixed number of alternatives does not prove that the design space has been explored.

Apply this judgment to novel interactions, architectural choices with multiple viable approaches, and product decisions where feel matters. Do not manufacture alternatives for mechanical changes, clear target states, or constraints that permit only one responsible answer.

Keep experiments and implementation within the authorized assignment.
